# Blood Bank Decision Support System — Data Foundation

This is the **data layer only** — no ML model, backend, frontend, or chatbot yet, per scope. Everything here exists to be a solid, honest, internally-consistent foundation for those later stages.

## The key architectural decision (read this first)

Your real macro time series (`supply_data.csv`/`demand_data.csv`, ~1,300 units/day system-wide) and your `donors.csv`/`donation_history.csv` files (~5.7 units/day, classified synthetic/provenance-to-be-confirmed — see Provenance table above) are **not the same system at two granularities** — they differ in scale by 150–200x. They represent a national/regional reporting feed and one branch's own donor roster, respectively.

This matters because it would have been easy to quietly force a fake reconciliation between them (e.g., pretend the 8,000 donors account for the entire national supply). I didn't do that. Instead:

- **`supply_data`/`demand_data`/`inventory_data`** stay exactly as provided, at their real system-wide granularity. They're the right input for macro-level forecasting and shortage-risk detection later.
- **`donors`/`donation_history`** are treated as what they actually are — one branch's own donor registry (provided source data, provenance to be confirmed — see Provenance table above) — and are the anchor for everything unit-level (`blood_units`, `blood_requests`, `blood_issues`).
- **`blood_requests.csv`** total volume per blood group is derived from this branch's own supply (sum of `blood_units`, itself traced to `donation_history.csv`), scaled by the *real* national demand/supply utilization ratio per ABO group (e.g., AB nationally runs at 94.4% demand/supply — structurally the tightest group — and that same relative tightness is preserved at branch scale). The *day-to-day shape* — trend, seasonality, the real 2020 dip — is copied proportionally from the real `demand_data.csv` pattern. So the numbers are branch-scale from source-provided data, but nationally-real in temporal texture. Full method in the validation report.

## Assumptions that need your (or a professional's) sign-off before this goes near production

1. **Rh distribution (94% +/ 6% −)** — drawn from a published systematic review of Indian blood donors, applied uniformly across ABO groups. Replace with your actual registry's measured Rh distribution.
2. **`units_donated = 2` in ~7% of `donation_history.csv` rows** — not standard for a single whole-blood donation (that's normally 1 unit; 2 usually implies apheresis, which needs different equipment and isn't distinguished in your source data). I generated 2 physical units for these rows at face value. **This needs a blood-bank professional to confirm what these records actually represent** before you trust `blood_units.csv` counts for those donations.
3. **42-day shelf life** — standard CPDA-1 whole blood/RBC storage limit. Doesn't apply to platelets (5 days) or FFP (up to 1 year frozen). This dataset models whole blood/RBC only.
4. **`priority` field (Routine/Urgent/Emergency)** — a random heuristic draw (70/22/8%), purely so the data has *something* to demonstrate priority-ranked allocation logic. It is **not** derived from any clinical triage protocol. Do not treat it as a real priority signal.
5. **Compatibility rules** cover whole blood/packed RBC only. Plasma and platelet compatibility follow different (in plasma's case, reverse) logic and are explicitly not included — see the `source_note` column in `compatibility_rules.csv`.
6. **`Quality Hold` discards (1.5% heuristic rate)** — a placeholder non-expiry discard reason so the data isn't 100% "just expiry," since real-world blood banks do reject units for other reasons. Not derived from any actual QC failure-rate data. Calibrate against real lab logs later.
7. **Phone numbers are Malaysian-format** in the original `donors.csv`. Left untouched — it's cosmetic and doesn't affect any logic, and manufacturing "more Indian-looking" fake numbers would just be effort spent on the wrong thing.

## What I deliberately did *not* do

- Did not modify `supply_data.csv`, `demand_data.csv`, or `inventory_data.csv` in any way.
- Did not fabricate a false row-level link between the national aggregate series and the branch-level donor data.
- Did not invent any clinical rule beyond standard, citable ABO/Rh RBC compatibility.
- Did not add patient names, hospital names, or any other unnecessary identifying fields to `blood_requests.csv`.
- Did not force `blood_units.csv`/`inventory_data.csv` discard rates to match exactly — see the validation report for why, and what the ~12.7% vs ~5.4% gap actually tells you.
